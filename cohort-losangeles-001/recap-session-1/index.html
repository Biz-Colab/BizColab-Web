The common thread

Six different businesses, six different challenges — but listen closely and they rhyme. Everyone described a version of “I need more clients.” Underneath, it’s one of two things — and neither is a lead problem.

Thread one

The offer isn’t decided yet

They can do many things well — but haven’t committed to the one thing: what exactly they sell, to whom, at what price. Until that’s decided, no website, ad, or referral can explain them — because there’s nothing crisp to repeat. The fix starts with a decision, not a marketing campaign.

RoyRoy said it himself: “I'm having a hard time packaging the value and communicating it clearly enough.”ReneeRenee's own words: “I need something sexy” — a two-word hook people instantly get and repeat.SvetlanaSvetlana: “We have strategies to reach customers — we just haven't implemented or tested them yet.”SudeepSudeep is still defining the venture itself — corp structure, the pitch, who the customer is — while working up the courage of the ask.
Thread two

The buyer isn’t chosen yet

The business already works — real clients, real revenue. But it grew by saying yes to everyone, so it never had to pick its best customer. The cost shows up later: expensive acquisition, deals stalling with the person who actually signs. The fix is choosing who to double down on.

AlexAlex: “It's not more clients — it's attracting more of the types of clients for whom churn is not an issue.”AmyAmy: risk managers use it and love it — but the CFOs and C-suite who actually sign are the ones she can't reach yet.
