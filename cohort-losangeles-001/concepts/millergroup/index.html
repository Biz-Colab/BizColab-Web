01 — First, a definition

What’s a challenger brand?

Not the smallest brand in the category — the most outnumbered one. The #2 (or #7) taking on a leader with ten times the budget, and ambition the media plan can’t keep up with.

Outspent

The category leader owns the airwaves. You can’t buy your way in — so the idea has to do the heavy lifting.

Outnumbered

Their marketing department is a floor of a building. Yours fits in a room — which means decisions ship in days, not quarters.

Underestimated

Retail, media and the market treat you as a footnote. Good. Nobody sees you coming.

Bring us what’s not working yet.

  • Maybe the market has changed and your message has not.
  • Maybe the data shows something is wrong but cannot tell you why.
  • Maybe you have a strong product, a committed team and a story people are not hearing.
  • Or maybe you are simply too close to the problem to see what your customers see.

That is where we do our best work — and have, across more than 250 engagements for challenger brands and social impact organizations since 1990.

Why this section?You asked for “challenger brand” to be defined — who they are and why. The current site uses the phrase only on the About page and never defines it, so the visitor has to already know the jargon. Defining it in the visitor’s own situation (outspent, outnumbered, underestimated) doubles as qualification: the right client recognizes themselves, the wrong one moves on. The “Maybe…” lines and the 250+ figure come from your own copy — now stated as engagements rather than brands, which is your word and the safer claim — they qualify the visitor in your voice, and they give the section the one number the current homepage never had. “Social impact organizations” is now named, because two of the four case studies below are exactly that.
02 — How we work

Ask. Aim. Make.

Every engagement starts with Discovery, and one senior team stays close from the first question through final execution — not three departments, but one loop.

ASK

Discovery

StreetPulse and stakeholder research. Real consumers, on camera, before a dollar goes to media — so strategy starts from evidence, not opinion.

AIM

Strategy

Positioning and the value story — the argument for your brand that survives a board meeting and a shelf test.

MAKE

Creative

Brand, campaigns and content built from what consumers actually said — not from what a brainstorm hoped.

At Gelson’s, the loop ran whole: consumers revealed a missing value story, strategy reframed it, creative shipped it — and sales rose 6% year over year.

Why this section?Your Session-1 steer for Renee: define the combined research + strategy + creative offer “in a clever, powerful way.” The current site lists six flat services; nothing says they compound. Three words name the loop (echoing her own “Ask Gelson’s” campaign), each stage carries a color, and the closing line proves the whole loop with her published number. ASK is now labelled Discovery — her own word for the first step — so the loop names her existing process instead of replacing it.
