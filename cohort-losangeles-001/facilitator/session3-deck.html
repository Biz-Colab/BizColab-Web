Your block

Nine minutes. Three beats.

0:00–1:30
Set it up — here's the artifact, here's the decision I'm stuck on, here's what I've already tried. No origin story.
1:30–7:30
The room works it — six minutes, no speaking order. Rewrites, numbers, warnings, names, hands. You mostly listen and take it down.
7:30–9:00
You call it — out loud: what you're changing, what you're dropping, and what you'll have done by Friday.

That last 90 seconds is the point of the whole night. A block that ends in "lots to think about" doesn't count — make the call while the room is still on the line.

How to be useful

Hands, not opinions.

Rewrite it, don't critique it

"That headline is weak" is worth nothing. Say the better headline out loud and let them type it.

Bring the number you know

What you actually paid, charged, converted or waited. Real figures beat instincts every time.

Name the name

"I know someone" isn't help. "I'll text Dana tonight" is. Say it in the room so it's on the record.

Say the hard thing early

If you think the whole approach is wrong, minute two — not minute eight. Kindly, and once.

Don't hijack the block

It's their nine minutes. If your story doesn't change their decision, save it for Slack.

Pass the mic

Talked twice and someone hasn't talked once? Stop and hand it over. I'll be doing this too.
