What tonight is

Build them something.

The bar for tonight isn't a good conversation. It's that your partner leaves with an object — something that exists now and didn't at 5:30.

Not this

A nice chat

  • "That's really interesting, have you thought about…"
  • Trading context for 14 minutes and running out of time
  • "Let's set something up" — the thing that never happens
  • Advice you could have given a stranger
This

A deliverable

  • A teardown — their site, pitch, pricing, funnel, pulled apart in writing
  • A plan — the next five steps, in order, with owners
  • An intro — a real name, and you send it this week
  • A solution — the thing they're stuck on, unstuck, in the room

You get 14 minutes on theirs and 14 on yours. Whatever you make gets shown to the room after the break.

Round the room

Two minutes. Three lines.

01 · ~40 sec

What you did

  • The actual thing you moved, not the thinking you did
  • If nothing moved, say that — it's more useful than a summary
02 · ~40 sec

Where the number is

  • Week 1's number, and today's
  • Flat is fine. Flat with a reason is better
03 · ~40 sec

What's in the way

  • The one blocker your partner should spend tonight on
  • Name it now so the pair room doesn't burn ten minutes finding it

Hard timer. No responses during the round — the pair rooms and the report-back are where the work happens.

In the pair room

Fourteen minutes, then switch.

Min 0–3

Get to the real constraint

  • Ask, don't advise. Three questions before you offer anything
  • "What have you already tried?" saves the other eleven minutes
Min 3–11

Make the thing

  • Screen share. Open the doc, the site, the deck, the spreadsheet
  • Write it down as you go — theirs to keep, not yours to remember
Min 11–14

Land it

  • What they'll actually do with it, and by when
  • What you owe them after tonight — the intro, the file, the name

The timer switches you at 14 minutes whether you're finished or not. Both halves get the same time — that's the whole deal.

How we give feedback

Direct beats polite.

Useless

Sounds supportive, does nothing

  • "I love this, I think you're really onto something"
  • "You should do more marketing"
  • "Have you thought about AI?"
  • Softening it so much the point disappears
Usable

Specific enough to act on tomorrow

  • "Your pricing page buries the number — move it above the fold"
  • "I ran that exact play at C/C/G and it took nine months, not six weeks"
  • "I'll intro you to [name] Thursday — she's hired for this twice"
  • Experience over advice: what happened when you tried it

Nobody paid $99 to be told their idea is nice. Say the thing you'd want said to you — and before we close a block, I'll call on whoever hasn't spoken yet.
