Run the week

Three standing prompts. Copy one, paste it in, done — the cohort context comes from project memory, so you never retype the setup. Everything that repeats gets written down once and never typed again.

Three patterns worth stealing
1–3–1“One problem: X. Three options with the tradeoff on each, then your recommendation and why. Under 200 words.”
The reverse brief“Before you build it, tell me back what you think I’m asking for and what you need from me. Then wait.”
Name the failure mode“The way this usually goes wrong is X. Prevent that specifically.”
Why this works

Write the prompt once. Then only ever type the trigger.

The camcorder method says: record yourself doing the thing once, hand the recording over, never do it again. The version here is the same move — a repeated job becomes a saved skill, and after that you say four words instead of four paragraphs. Recaps, page patches and reminder texts are all replaceable and repeatable. The 90 minutes in the room is not.

Two rules underneath it. Context beats cleverness — say who it is for, what it replaces, and what done looks like. And ask for the diff, not the doc — “patch the live page” is cheap and safe, “rebuild the page” is how work gets silently reverted.
